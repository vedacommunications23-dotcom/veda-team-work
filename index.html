Knowledge Centre

Practical CIBIL education for customers — written for Indian credit reports and lending practice.

What is a CIBIL score?

A three-digit number (typically 300–900) summarising your credit history for lenders. Higher scores usually mean better loan/card eligibility and rates.

Score bands (practical view)

Below 650 is weak for most unsecured credit; 650–699 is borderline; 700–749 is workable for many products; 750+ is generally preferred by prime lenders.

Overdue vs written-off

Overdue means unpaid dues on an open account. Written-off means the lender has treated the debt as a loss — it remains a serious negative even after settlement in many cases.

Settled accounts

“Settled” often means you paid less than the full outstanding. It can still restrict approvals with strict lenders compared to a clean closed status.

DPD / delay history

Days Past Due shows how late payments were. Even closed accounts can carry historical DPD that lenders review.

Guarantor / joint liability

If you are a guarantor or joint holder, that exposure can affect your profile even when you are not the primary borrower.

When to dispute

Dispute wrong identity matches, accounts not yours, incorrect balances, or already-paid dues still showing overdue — with proof (statements, NOC, payment receipts).

Documents we usually need

Full name, email, mobile, PAN, DOB, pincode/address, and your latest CIBIL PDF when available. Extra proof helps for dispute cases.

Enquiries

Too many recent loan/card enquiries can temporarily pressure score and lender confidence. Apply only when needed.

How our analysis helps

We map open/closed negatives, prioritise what to fix first, and track case status with you until informed/closed — including partner-referred clients.
